What he does

Keeps an eye on Claude

He watches claude.ai in your browser, the Claude app and Claude Code, and acts it out: typing away while Claude works, waving when it's waiting on you, celebrating when it's done.

Chats with you

Press ⌥ Space from any app (or double-click him) and ask anything, by typing or dictating. Drop a PDF on him, or press his screen button to show him what you're looking at. He searches the web, finds your files, and reads your Gmail, Calendar, Reminders and Notes.

Keeps your day on track

A short rundown when you sit down in the morning, an offer to prep you ten minutes before each meeting, and a wrap-up at the end of the day. Tell him who or what to watch for in your inbox, and he holds up a letter when it arrives. When a long Claude Code job finishes, he tells you what got done.

Asks before he changes anything

Adding an event, a reminder or a note, drafting an email or running a shortcut shows you exactly what he'll do, with Yes and No. Nothing happens until you say yes, and he never sends email.

Knows when to step aside

He hides while you share your screen or present, keeps his news to himself during meetings and focus sessions, and puts a Join button up a minute before a call. Press ⌃⌥ C anywhere to jot a reminder without opening anything.

Keeps an eye on things

Stretch breaks after a long stretch at the keyboard, a heads-up when your battery, disk or Downloads need attention, a wave when one of your websites goes down, and an "It's live!" flag when your release goes out. Put music on and he gets the boombox out.

Remembers what you tell him

Say "remember that I prefer meetings after 10" and he keeps it, once you OK it. A few questions the first time he opens, and What He Knows About You shows it all.

Wakes up when you come over

He dozes when nothing's going on. Click him or rest your pointer on him and he yawns, rubs his eyes and waves, and he watches the bubble while you type.

Keeps himself up to date

When there's a new version he says so in his chat, and one click installs it. He works on company networks too, and Report a Problem shows you what it sends first.

What you need

  • A Mac with macOS 12 Monterey or later. Apple silicon and Intel both work.
  • Claude Pro or Max. His chat runs on Claude Code, which comes with those plans. On the free plan he still reacts to Claude, but he can't chat.
  • That's it. He installs Claude Code and signs you in himself, so there's no Terminal involved.

Where your stuff goes

Fox Buddy is a front end for Claude Code. Everything he thinks with goes through Claude Code, under your own Claude account and Anthropic's terms. There's no Fox Buddy server, no account to make, and no analytics in the app.

Goes to Anthropic, through Claude Code

Your chats with him, and whatever he looks at to answer: a file you let him open or drop on him, a picture of your screen you chose to send, a note, your reminders. His check-ins, meeting prep and recaps of long Claude Code jobs go the same way. Gmail and Calendar go through your claude.ai connectors, the same way they do on claude.ai. Claude Code also sends Anthropic its own usage and error reports.

Goes to GitHub

Once a day he asks whether there's a newer version of him, and updates download from there. Nothing about you is in either. If you ask him to watch a GitHub repository or a website, he checks those directly too, and the same goes for a calendar link or Slack in his settings; out of the box, he doesn't.

Stays on your Mac

His settings, what he knows about you, his logs, your email alerts and upcoming meetings, in a folder in your home folder. Quick captures go to Reminders or a file in your Documents, and birthdays (if you turn them on) are read from Contacts on your Mac. To know when you're sharing your screen, he looks at which windows are open, never what's in them. He clears his chat history when you start a new chat. His parts only talk to each other on your Mac, and the browser extension tells him that Claude is working, never what you typed.

What he can't do

  • Run commands, or change, move or delete your files. His chats run Claude Code with those switched off, and a gate checks every single thing he tries against his list.
  • Send email. He can write a Gmail draft, after you say yes.
  • Make a change on his own. An event, a draft, a reminder, a note or a shortcut only happens once you approve that exact change, and only once.
  • Look at your screen on his own. He takes a picture only when you press his screen button, and it goes nowhere until you send a message. You can remove it first.
  • Open your files unless you let him. He finds them by name. Reading inside one takes a click, or dropping it on him, and lasts for that chat.

What macOS will ask you about

  • Reminders and Notes, the first time he uses them. You can turn them off in his menu.
  • Screen Recording, the first time you press his screen button.
  • Contacts, only if you turn on birthdays.
  • Accessibility, only if you switch on watching the Claude desktop app.
  • A helper that starts at login, which keeps him in touch with Claude Code. Setup also adds a few lines to Claude Code's settings so your sessions can tell him what they're doing.

Using him at work? He uses whichever Claude account Claude Code is signed in to, so check that it's one your company is OK with before pointing him at work files or email. Report a Problem shows you exactly what it would send before anything goes anywhere. This website, not the app, uses Google Tag Manager to count visits.

Install him

  1. Download the DMG and open it.
  2. Drag Fox Buddy into Applications.
  3. Open him and click Set Up, then answer a few quick questions so he knows what to call you.

Or, with Homebrew:

brew tap justinfuchs85/fox-buddy https://github.com/justinfuchs85/fox-buddy
brew install --cask justinfuchs85/fox-buddy/fox-buddy
